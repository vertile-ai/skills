# AGLC4 essentials

The *Australian Guide to Legal Citation* (4th edition, Melbourne University Law Review Association and Melbourne Journal of International Law, 2018) is the controlling text. Rule numbers and printed page numbers below were checked against the official PDF (2021 file `AGLC4-2021-v1.pdf`) linked from the publisher's AGLC page. See `[AGLC4]` in [sources.md](sources.md). AGLC4 is still the current edition. Consultation on AGLC5 has closed, so check the publisher's page before relying on these rules for a long-lived document.

Examples marked *(AGLC4)* are taken from the Guide's own examples. The other examples were checked against the source itself.

## Where AGLC4 applies

- Use it for advice, memos, research notes, academic work, and anything where the reader expects formal Australian citation.
- Court documents follow the court's practice notes and the forum's usual practice. Do not convert them to AGLC4 just to be tidy.
- In letters, identify the authority plainly (name, citation, pinpoint) in the text. Footnotes are rarely needed.

## Footnotes and pinpoints

| Rule | What it says | Page |
|---|---|---|
| 1.1.1 | Footnote authority for propositions and direct quotations (unless the source is given in full in the text). Give the first citation in full. | 1 |
| 1.1.3 | Separate several sources in one footnote with semicolons, not "and". A new signal starts a new sentence. | 3 |
| 1.1.4 | End every footnote with a full stop or other closing punctuation. | 3 |
| 1.1.6 | A pinpoint follows the citation directly, with no "p" or "pg", and no "at" except under rule 1.4.6. Cite case paragraphs as `[23]`, not "para 23". Legislation uses "para" for paragraphs of a section. Use page then paragraph: `6 [23]`. | 4 |
| 1.1.7 | Spans use an en dash: `431–2`, `[57]–[63]`. | 6 |
| 1.2 | Signals: none (direct support), See, See, eg, See also, See especially, See generally, Cf, But see. A new signal in the same footnote starts a new sentence. | 7 |

## Subsequent references

| Rule | What it says | Page |
|---|---|---|
| 1.4.1 | Cite a source already cited by a short form and a cross-reference: cases and legislation use the short title, *Penfolds Wines* (n 53) 224 (Dixon J); most secondary sources use the author's surname, MacMillan (n 48) 41 *(AGLC4, examples on p 10)*. "Above n" is no longer used for this. | 9 |
| 1.4.3 | `Ibid` refers only to the source in the immediately preceding footnote, and only if that footnote has one source. Capitalise it at the start of a footnote. Same pinpoint: `Ibid.` Different pinpoint: `Ibid 162.` with no comma. If the previous footnote had a pinpoint and the new reference is general, use `(n X)`, not `Ibid` *(AGLC4)*. | 11 |
| 1.4.4 | Introduce a short title after the first full citation: `(‘Short Title’)`. Italicise it if the full title is italicised. Give the full title at the first reference in the text too; if a short title was introduced only in a footnote, introduce it again in the text when first used there. | 12 |
| 1.4.6 | Within the same footnote, use "at" for a later pinpoint to the immediately preceding source. Never use "ibid" within one footnote. | 14 |

Re-check every `Ibid` and `(n X)` after footnotes are added, deleted, or moved.

## Quotations

| Rule | What it says | Page |
|---|---|---|
| 1.5.1 | Use single quotation marks for quotations of three lines or less. Set a quotation of four lines or more as an indented block, in smaller type, without quotation marks. Use double marks for a quotation inside a short quotation. | 15 |
| 1.5.3 | Mark omissions with a spaced ellipsis ( … ). Do not start a quotation with an ellipsis. | 18 |
| 1.5.4 | Show alterations in square brackets. | 18 |
| 1.5.5 | Use [sic] for a significant error in the original (it may also mark discriminatory or offensive language), not for spelling variants. | 19 |
| 1.5.7 | Add `(emphasis added)` or `(emphasis in original)` after the citation where relevant. | 21 |

## Cases

Template for a reported case: *Case Name* (Year) Volume Report Page, Pinpoint (Judge).

| Rule | What it says | Page |
|---|---|---|
| 2.1.1 | Use the parties' names as on the first page of the decision, in italics. Use surnames only for individuals, the first plaintiff and first defendant only, and no "& Anor" or "& Ors". | 39 |
| 2.1.2 | Corporate abbreviations: Pty, Ltd, Co, Inc, &, (in liq). Omit "trading as" and former names. Omit the ACN unless there is no other name. | 40 |
| 2.1.13 | Where the number appears in the case name, put it in square brackets: [No 2], not "(No 2)". | 46 |
| 2.1.14 | A popular or shortened case name can be a short title for later references. | 46 |
| 2.2.1 | Use round brackets for the year if the series is organised by volume number, e.g. `(1992) 175 CLR 1`. Use square brackets if the year identifies the volume, e.g. `[1976] 2 NSWLR 72` *(AGLC4)*. | 49 |
| 2.2.2 | Prefer, in order: authorised report (CLR, FCR, SASR, NSWLR, VR …), generalist unauthorised report (ALR, ALJR …), subject-specific report, medium neutral citation, unreported. | 50 |
| 2.2.3 | Do not italicise report series abbreviations. | 51 |
| 2.2.5 | Put a comma before the page pinpoint, and repeat the first page if that is the pinpoint: `24 CLR 21, 21` *(AGLC4)*. | 52 |
| 2.2.7 | No parallel citations for Australian cases. | 54 |
| 2.3.1 | Unreported with a medium neutral citation allocated by the court itself: *Case Name* [Year] Court Number, [Pinpoint]. Pinpoints are paragraph numbers. The Guide's table gives the years each court allocated numbers (High Court: 1998 onwards). Retrospective database identifiers are not court-allocated. | 54 |
| 2.3.2 | Unreported without a court-allocated medium neutral citation: *Case Name* (Court, Judge(s), Full Date) Pinpoint. | 56 |
| 2.4.1 | When using a pinpoint, generally name the judge after it in parentheses, e.g. (Dixon J), unless it is otherwise apparent. Do not use "per". | 58 |

Checked examples:

- *Mabo v Queensland [No 2]* (1992) 175 CLR 1, decided 3 June 1992. The report heading reads "Mabo and Others v. Queensland (No. 2)"; under rules 2.1.1 and 2.1.13 this becomes *Mabo v Queensland [No 2]* `[CASE-MABO]`. AustLII lists it as [1992] HCA 23. That is a retrospective database identifier, not a court-allocated citation, so never add it.
- *Penfolds Wines Pty Ltd v Elliott* (1946) 74 CLR 204, decided 25 November 1946 `[CASE-PENFOLDS]`. AGLC4 uses it as its short-title example (p 10): *Penfolds Wines Pty Ltd v Elliott* (1946) 74 CLR 204 (‘*Penfolds Wines*’), then *Penfolds Wines* (n 53) 224 (Dixon J).
- Medium neutral form *(AGLC4 p 5)*: *Agius v South Australia [No 6]* [2018] FCA 358, [90]–[97] (Mortimer J).

## Legislation

Template: *Title Year* (Jurisdiction) Pinpoint.

| Rule | What it says | Page |
|---|---|---|
| 3.1.1–3.1.2 | Use the short title and the year of original enactment, in italics. Generally cite the principal Act as amended; cite an amending Act only where the amendment itself matters (rule 3.8). | 67–68 |
| 3.1.3 | Jurisdiction in roman type in parentheses: Cth, ACT, NSW, NT, Qld, SA, Tas, Vic, WA. | 68 |
| 3.1.4 | Pinpoint abbreviations: `s`/`ss`, `sub-s`, `pt`, `div`, `sch`, `cl`, `para`, `ch`. Use `s 5(1)`, not `s5(1)` or `sub-s 5(1)`. Use `pt 3 div 2` with no comma between levels. Do not add the part when citing a section. | 69 |
| 3.1.5 | Multiple pinpoints: `ss 198, 198AB`; spans with an en dash: `sub-ss (2)–(3)`. | 71 |
| 3.1.6 | Unnumbered definition: `s 9 (definition of ‘administrator’)`. | 72 |
| 3.1.7 | Part of an Act with its own name: *Competition and Consumer Act 2010* (Cth) sch 2 (‘*Australian Consumer Law*’) *(AGLC4)*, then *Australian Consumer Law* (n X) s 18 (adapted; the Guide's example uses s 3). | 73 |
| 3.5 | Short titles for Acts: `Administrative Decisions (Judicial Review) Act 1977 (Cth) (‘ADJR Act’)`, then `ADJR Act (n 46) s 7`. `Ibid` works as usual: `Ibid s 6` *(AGLC4)*. | 75 |
| 3.6 | `Australian Constitution s 51(ii)`. Cite state constitutions as ordinary statutes, e.g. `Constitution Act 1934 (SA) s 5` *(AGLC4)*. | 76 |

Checked examples:

- *Acts Interpretation Act 1901* (Cth) s 15AA (interpretation that best achieves the Act's purpose) `[AIA]`.
- *Legislation Interpretation Act 2021* (SA) s 14 (interpretation that best achieves the purpose or object) `[SA-LIA]`.
- *Competition and Consumer Act 2010* (Cth) sch 2 s 18 (misleading or deceptive conduct) `[ACL]`.
- *Privacy Act 1988* (Cth) sch 1 cl 5 (APP 5, notification of collection) `[PRIV]`. In prose, "APP 5" and "APP 5.2" are the usual names. For a pinpoint below clause level, follow the decimal-numbering note in rule 3.1.4 and state the form you chose.

## Journal articles

Template: Author, ‘Title’ (Year) Volume(Issue) Journal Starting Page, Pinpoint.

- Example *(AGLC4 ch 5)*: Harold Luntz, ‘A Personal Journey through the Law of Torts’ (2005) 27(3) *Sydney Law Review* 393, 400.
- Include the issue number or other issue identifier (rule 5.4). Put the title in single quotation marks and italicise the journal name. Omit post-nominals such as QC (rule 5.1). Leave no spaces between initials (rule 4.1.1).
- Subsequent reference: `Luntz (n X) 401.`

## Common slips

- Using "(No 2)" instead of `[No 2]`.
- Adding a medium neutral citation as a parallel citation to a reported case, or treating a retrospective database identifier (e.g. pre-1998 "HCA" numbers) as a court-allocated citation.
- Swapping round and square brackets for the year.
- Missing the jurisdiction after an Act, or italicising it.
- Writing "at 42", "p 42", or "para 42" for a case pinpoint.
- Using `Ibid` after a footnote that cited two sources.
- Using "above n" for cross-references to earlier footnotes (AGLC3 style).
- Guessing a report series abbreviation. Check it in AGLC4 appendix A; if the series is not listed, write its name in full (rule 2.2.3).
- Copying a citation from a secondary source without opening the primary source.
