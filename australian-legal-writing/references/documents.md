# Document guides

Read the section for the document you are drafting. Bracketed tags point to [sources.md](sources.md). South Australian rules are given because SA is the default jurisdiction. In any other jurisdiction, find that court's equivalent rule; do not reuse the SA rule.

## Letters and correspondence

- Write as if the letter will be shown to a judge. Be courteous, firm, and specific.
- Put the request or answer in the first paragraph. Use one topic per paragraph and dated facts, and list only the enclosures you actually attach.
- Do not make an admission, offer, concession, undertaking, or threat the user has not instructed.
- "Without prejudice" protection depends on substance, not the label: the communication must be connected with a genuine attempt to settle a dispute, and the statutory exceptions still apply. A demand that makes no offer may still be connected with negotiation, so do not promise either way. The rules differ by jurisdiction; in courts applying the *Evidence Act 1995* (Cth), see s 131 and its exceptions `[EVID]`. Use the label only on instruction.

## Letter of demand

Include:

1. The parties, with the debtor's correct legal name. For a company, use its name and ACN as shown on the ASIC register.
2. The basis of the claim: contract, invoice numbers, dates, goods or services supplied.
3. The amount, how it is calculated, and any interest. Claim a contractual interest rate only if the contract provides one. Otherwise say the claimant may seek interest from the court: for SA Magistrates Court money judgments, the court will award pre-judgment interest on application unless good reason is shown, at a rate it fixes. For a liquidated claim the period runs from the due date to judgment unless the court decides otherwise `[SA-MCA s 34]`. Do not invent a rate.
4. A clear deadline and how to pay or respond.
5. The next step, limited to what the client is entitled and instructed to do.
6. Copies of the documents the recipient needs to check the claim.

Rules that commonly apply:

- **Which SA track?** A minor civil action is founded on a small claim (a monetary claim of $12,000 or less), a neighbourhood dispute, or a minor statutory proceeding. Some of the last two stay minor civil actions above $12,000 unless a party elects otherwise `[SA-MCA s 3(2)–(4a)]`. Classify the claim, then pick the route.
- **SA minor civil action, notice of intention.** Serve it at least 21 days before starting. It must give the claimant's full name and address for service, identify the claim and relief in enough detail for the respondent to decide whether to admit it and make an offer, attach any relevant expert report, and, for personal injury, include the r 61.7(2) details `[UCR r 332.2(1)]`. Personal injury also needs an earlier notice of injury within 6 months of the incident (extended where the person was unaware) unless the claimant has already given a *Motor Vehicles Act 1959* s 126A notice or made a *Return to Work Act 2014* s 30 claim in time; confirm the actual steps and dates, and confirm compliance in a minor civil statement of claim `[UCR r 332.1, 61.6]`. The respondent must then reply within 21 days with the content in r 332.3(1), including an offer `[UCR r 332.3]`.
- **SA minor civil action, Final Notice.** For matters other than personal injury, a filed and served Final Notice (Form P1) is the alternative. If you intend to rely on an expert report, serve every expert report relevant to the proposed action with the Final Notice. The respondent need not reply in writing `[UCR r 332.2(2), 332.3(3)]`, but the form gives them 21 days to respond or pay, so allow 21 days after service before filing `[SA-FORM-P1]`.
- **No notice needed** in the listed cases, e.g. certain injunction applications, enforcing a binding determination, a claim for a claimed amount under ss 15 or 16 of the *Building and Construction Industry Security of Payment Act 2009* (SA), or a statutory time limit of 3 months or less `[UCR r 332.2(3)]`. Skipping a required notice can cost the claimant costs, including a filing fee `[UCR r 332.2(4)]`. The SA Law Handbook gives a practical walkthrough `[SA-LAWHB]`.
- **SA general claims** need a fuller pre-action claim: how the amount is calculated, an offer to settle, a costs estimate (Form P3, except for personal injury), and a proposed pre-action meeting `[UCR r 61.7]`. Exemptions are in r 61.8.
- **SA pre-action confidentiality.** Unless the parties agree otherwise, communications in or for pre-action steps are confidential and subject to settlement privilege, including in minor civil actions. Under r 61.4(2), the court can direct disclosure only for limited procedural or costs orders (and not to the trial judge before liability and quantum are decided), subject to general-law and statutory settlement-privilege exceptions (see the note to r 61.4) `[UCR r 61.4, 331.3(h)]`. Do not plan to show the demand or reply to the trial judge.
- **Debt collection conduct.** Explain the consequences of non-payment accurately and do not misrepresent them. Do not threaten legal action that is not possible, not intended, or not instructed `[RG96 s 19(b)–(c)]`. Take extra care with consumers and vulnerable debtors.
- **A statutory demand is not a letter of demand.** A creditor's statutory demand to a company has prescribed content and form, a statutory period, and usually a supporting affidavit `[CORP s 459E]`. Its consequences are serious. Use the official form and recommend legal review.

## Advice and memos

Use this order: the answer in two or three sentences, the question, the facts and assumptions, the law with pinpoints, how the law applies, risks and options, and next steps.

- State the jurisdiction and "as at" date.
- Separate what the law clearly says from your reading of it. Match your confidence to the strength of each point.
- List open questions and anything marked `[VERIFY]` at the end, not scattered through the text.
- Cite in AGLC4 unless the reader uses a different house style ([aglc4.md](aglc4.md)).

## Submissions

The order is in SKILL.md. Also:

- Use only facts that are in evidence, cited to the place in the record (affidavit paragraph, exhibit, or transcript page and line).
- Deal with the other side's best point and concede what must be conceded. Overstatement costs credibility.
- Follow the court's practice notes on length, format, and citation. Check whether the court requires a list of authorities.
- **Generative AI.** In the NSW Supreme Court, where Gen AI helped prepare written submissions or summaries or skeletons of argument, the author must verify in the body of the document that all citations, authorities, and legislative references exist, are accurate, and are relevant, and do the same for references to evidence. That check must not be done solely with a Gen AI tool `[NSW-PN23 paras 16–17]`. Do not put material covered by para 9A (non-publication or suppression orders, the Harman undertaking, subpoenaed material, or a statutory publication ban) into a Gen AI tool unless the practice note's conditions are met `[NSW-PN23 para 9A]`. Check the forum's current rules before drafting.

## Affidavits

- An affidavit is the deponent's evidence. Use first person, numbered paragraphs, and facts within the deponent's own knowledge. Do not include argument or legal conclusions.
- Hearsay is allowed only where the rules permit it. In SA, each hearsay statement in a permitted affidavit must say the deponent believes it, identify its source (the person or document), and make clear it is first-hand hearsay `[UCR r 31.7(12)]`.
- Use the court's prescribed form. In SA courts governed by the Uniform Civil Rules 2020, an affidavit must be in Form 12, give the deponent's address and occupation, be signed and witnessed by an authorised person, and have every page dated and signed `[UCR r 31.7]`.
- **Generative AI limits.** In the NSW Supreme Court, Gen AI must not be used to generate or rephrase the content of affidavits or witness statements, and the document must disclose that Gen AI was not used for that content `[NSW-PN23 paras 10–13]`. The disclosure also covers annexures and exhibits the deponent prepared for their evidence (not those created outside the proceedings), and Gen AI may be used for them only with leave in exceptional cases `[NSW-PN23 paras 13(b), 14–15]`. SA courts have generative AI guidelines that apply from 1 January 2026 `[SA-GENAI]`. Before helping with any affidavit, check the forum's current AI rules. Where AI drafting is barred, limit help to structure, a checklist, or editing for form, and say why.
- A statutory declaration is a different document under a different Act. Use the regime the requesting body requires, and copy the prescribed words exactly.

## Contract clauses

- Use one obligation per clause: party, "must" or "may", and the action `[OPC-PEM para 83]`. Add a time limit or consequence only where the parties agreed one; otherwise ask.
- Define money (currency, GST inclusive or exclusive, when payable) and time (business or calendar days, whose public holidays, inclusive or exclusive).
- Keep recitals non-operative; anything that must bind goes in an operative clause.
- Know what boilerplate does (governing law, notices, entire agreement, variation, assignment, severance, counterparts) before cutting it.
- **Standard form contracts** with consumers or small businesses: an unfair term is void, and entering into such a contract containing an unfair term you proposed, or applying, relying on, or purporting to apply or rely on one, is a contravention that can attract a penalty `[ACL s 23(1), (2A), (2C)]`. Check penalties and coverage on the ACCC contracts page `[ACCC-CONTRACTS]`.
- **Consumer guarantees** cannot be excluded `[ACL s 64]`. For goods or services not of a kind ordinarily acquired for personal, domestic, or household use, a supplier can sometimes limit liability to the remedies listed in s 64A, unless that is not fair or reasonable `[ACL s 64A]`. Never draft "no refunds" wording that implies otherwise `[ACCC-GUARANTEES]`.
- When rewriting legalese in plain English, keep every condition, exception, time limit, and party. List anything you could not carry over.

## Website and app legal text (first pass only)

- **Collection notice (APP 5).** At or before collection, take the steps reasonable in the circumstances to tell the person (or otherwise make them aware of) the APP 5.2 matters that are reasonable in the circumstances. Only if that is impracticable, do it as soon as practicable afterwards. Several apply only if their condition is met. The list: who you are and how to contact you, the facts of third-party or unexpected collection, any Australian law or court or tribunal order requiring or authorising collection (with the law's name or the order's details), the purposes, the consequences of not providing information, usual disclosures, that the privacy policy explains access, correction, and complaints, and any likely overseas disclosure and, if practicable, the countries `[PRIV sch 1 cl 5] [OAIC-APP5]`. Put the notice where collection happens.
- **Privacy policy (APP 1).** Follow the APP 1 contents and the OAIC guidance `[OAIC-APP1]`. From 10 December 2026, a policy must also cover certain automated decisions (APP 1.7–1.9) `[POLA-2024 s 2 item 7]`.
- **Coverage.** Many small businesses are exempt from the APPs, but the exemption has exceptions. Check *Privacy Act 1988* (Cth) s 6D and the related sections before assuming it applies `[PRIV s 6D]`.
- **Marketing email or SMS.** A commercial electronic message with an Australian link generally needs consent (express or inferred), accurate sender identification, and a working unsubscribe facility, subject to the Act's exceptions `[SPAM ss 16–18]`.
- **Claims and pricing.** Do not engage in misleading or deceptive conduct or make false or misleading representations `[ACL ss 18, 29]`.
